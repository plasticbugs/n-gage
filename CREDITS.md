# Credits

A core built from this template is a small amount of new work sitting on a
large amount of other people's. This file is the template's share of that debt;
a core should extend it with its own — MAME's driver authors, the vendored CPU
and sound cores, and whoever documented the board.

## Marcus Andrade — [@boogermann](https://github.com/boogermann), [OpenGateware](https://github.com/opengateware) / [Raetro](https://github.com/raetro)

**Everything between the arcade hardware and the Pocket is his**, and that is
most of what makes this template a working core rather than a directory of
ideas.

* **`platform/pocket/`** is OpenGateware's `gateman-pocket` platform: the APF
  bridge, the ROM and data loader, the video mixer and scaler, the audio path
  and its filters, Interact option decoding, the memory primitives, hiscore
  support and the pin constraints. **41 of its 63 files carry his copyright.**
* **`projects/`** — the Quartus project files were generated by his **Gateman**
  CLI.
* **`target/pocket/core_top.sv`** starts from his template; the half above the
  "@ The game" banner is still substantially his.
* **The build itself.** `build-local.sh`, `.github/workflows/compile.yml` and
  `projects/report_worst.tcl` all run **`raetro/quartus:pocket`**, his Docker
  image of Quartus Prime 18.1 Lite. Every core here is compiled with it,
  locally and in CI, on Apple silicon through `--platform linux/amd64`. Without
  it, building these cores would mean a hand-installed Quartus and a build that
  differs from machine to machine.

Licensing is mostly MIT, some GPL-3.0-or-later and CC0-1.0; **each file says**,
and the SPDX headers are authoritative.

## MAME

The reference this method depends on: MAME's drivers and device models are the
oracle every core here is checked against, and `ref/mame/` holds the exact
files a core was written from, fetched verbatim. Credit the specific driver's
authors in the core's own README and in `docs/hardware.md`.

## The house style

`METHODOLOGY.md` was written across the Xenophobe, Time Pilot, Gaiapolis,
Pleiads/Phoenix, Cadash and Master of Weapon cores, and this template was cut
from the last of them.

## When you add to this

Vendored CPU and sound cores (`modules/`) are recorded in `modules/VENDOR.md`
with their upstream, exact commit and licence — `tools/vendor.sh` prints the
row to paste. Keep that table filled in as you vendor them, and name their
authors in the core's README: fx68k is Jorge Cwik's, tv80 Guy Hutchison's, the
jt-series sound chips Jose Tejada's, and they are the reason a core can exist
at all. Never strip a licence header from a file you copy — the SPDX headers
in `platform/` and `modules/` are the licence, not decoration.
